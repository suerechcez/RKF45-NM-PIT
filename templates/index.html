Runge Kutta Fehlberg (RKF45)

Adaptive 4(5)-order ODE solver — solve $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = f(t, y)$

1. Mathematical Discussion

The Runge–Kutta–Fehlberg method (RKF45) solves the initial value problem $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = f(t, y),\ y(t_0) = y_0$ using an embedded pair of Runge–Kutta formulas. At every step, two approximations of $y(t_k+h)$ are computed simultaneously: $y_{k+1}$ of order 4 and $z_{k+1}$ of order 5. Both share the same six stage values $k_1,\ldots,k_6$, so the extra accuracy comes essentially for free.

The local truncation error of the 4th-order formula is estimated by $|z_{k+1} - y_{k+1}|$. If this error exceeds the user-supplied tolerance $\text{tol}$, the step is rejected and retried with a smaller $h$; otherwise it is accepted. In either case the step size for the next iteration is rescaled by $s \approx 0.84\,(\text{tol}\cdot h / |z_{k+1}-y_{k+1}|)^{1/4}$, producing an adaptive integrator that automatically tightens the mesh in stiff regions and loosens it where the solution is smooth. RKF45 is therefore well-suited to problems where the cost of evaluating $f$ dominates and uniform step methods would either over- or under-sample.

2. Worked Examples

Example 1 — $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = t + y,\ y(0)=1,\ h=0.5$

Evaluate one RKF45 step from $t_0=0$ to $t_1=0.5$. With $f(t,y)=t+y$:

$$ \begin{aligned} k_1 &= 0.5\,f(0,\,1) = 0.5000000000 \\ k_2 &= 0.5\,f(0.125,\,1.125) = 0.6250000000 \\ k_3 &= 0.5\,f(0.1875,\,1.22265625) = 0.7050781250 \\ k_4 &= 0.5\,f(0.46153846,\,1.73282\ldots) = 1.0972348657 \\ k_5 &= 0.5\,f(0.5,\,1.84815\ldots) = 1.1740785256 \\ k_6 &= 0.5\,f(0.25,\,1.30181\ldots) = 0.7759039463 \end{aligned} $$
$$ \begin{aligned} y_1 &= 1 + \tfrac{25}{216}k_1 + \tfrac{1408}{2565}k_3 + \tfrac{2197}{4104}k_4 - \tfrac{1}{5}k_5 = 1.7974759615 \\ z_1 &= 1 + \tfrac{16}{135}k_1 + \tfrac{6656}{12825}k_3 + \tfrac{28561}{56430}k_4 - \tfrac{9}{50}k_5 + \tfrac{2}{55}k_6 = 1.7974108574 \\ |z_1 - y_1| &= 6.51 \times 10^{-5} \end{aligned} $$

The exact solution is $y(t)=2e^{t}-t-1$, giving $y(0.5)=1.7974425414$. The 5th-order estimate $z_1$ differs from the true value by only $3.17\times 10^{-5}$, while the embedded error indicator $|z_1-y_1|=6.51\times 10^{-5}$ provides a reliable, slightly conservative bound — exactly as the theory predicts.

Example 2 — $\dfrac{dy}{dt} = y - t^{2} + 1,\ y(0)=0.5,\ h=0.25$

One RKF45 step from $t_0=0$ to $t_1=0.25$ with $f(t,y)=y-t^2+1$:

$$ \begin{aligned} k_1 &= 0.25\,f(0,\,0.5) = 0.3750000000 \\ k_2 &= 0.25\,f(0.0625,\,0.59375) = 0.3974609375 \\ k_3 &= 0.25\,f(0.09375,\,0.60872\ldots) = 0.4095382690 \\ k_4 &= 0.25\,f(0.23077,\,0.79899\ldots) = 0.4584970939 \\ k_5 &= 0.25\,f(0.25,\,0.83137\ldots) = 0.4658451569 \\ k_6 &= 0.25\,f(0.125,\,0.69838\ldots) = 0.4204789125 \end{aligned} $$
$$ \begin{aligned} y_1 &= 0.9204886021 \quad \text{(4th order)} \\ z_1 &= 0.9204870493 \quad \text{(5th order)} \\ |z_1 - y_1| &= 1.55 \times 10^{-6} \end{aligned} $$

The exact solution is $y(t)=(t+1)^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}e^{t}$, giving $y(0.25)=0.9204872917$. The 5th-order estimate is accurate to $2.4\times 10^{-7}$. Because the embedded error $1.55\times 10^{-6}$ is well below a typical tolerance of $10^{-4}$, the next step size would be increased by $s = 0.84\,(10^{-4}\cdot 0.25 / 1.55\times 10^{-6})^{1/4} \approx 2.4$.

(28) Stage values
$$ \begin{aligned} k_1 &= h\, f(t_k,\, y_k) \\ k_2 &= h\, f\!\left(t_k + \tfrac{1}{4}h,\; y_k + \tfrac{1}{4}k_1\right) \\ k_3 &= h\, f\!\left(t_k + \tfrac{3}{8}h,\; y_k + \tfrac{3}{32}k_1 + \tfrac{9}{32}k_2\right) \\ k_4 &= h\, f\!\left(t_k + \tfrac{12}{13}h,\; y_k + \tfrac{1932}{2197}k_1 - \tfrac{7200}{2197}k_2 + \tfrac{7296}{2197}k_3\right) \\ k_5 &= h\, f\!\left(t_k + h,\; y_k + \tfrac{439}{216}k_1 - 8 k_2 + \tfrac{3680}{513}k_3 - \tfrac{845}{4104}k_4\right) \\ k_6 &= h\, f\!\left(t_k + \tfrac{1}{2}h,\; y_k - \tfrac{8}{27}k_1 + 2 k_2 - \tfrac{3544}{2565}k_3 + \tfrac{1859}{4104}k_4 - \tfrac{11}{40}k_5\right) \end{aligned} $$
